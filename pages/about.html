India's Conservation Legacy

For over half a century, India has been at the forefront of wildlife conservation, enacting robust legal frameworks and launching ambitious projects to protect its priceless natural heritage. These initiatives form the backbone of the country's efforts to safeguard biodiversity.

Wildife Protection Act, 1972

The foundational legal framework for the protection of wild animals, birds, and plants. It prohibits the hunting of endangered species and established the network of National Parks and Sanctuaries.

Project Tiger, 1973

A landmark conservation program launched to save the Bengal tiger from extinction. It has grown into one of the most successful species-specific conservation stories in the world, creating a network of Tiger Reserves.

Project Elephant, 1992

A nationwide initiative to protect the Asian elephant, its habitat, and corridors. It aims to address issues of human-elephant conflict and ensure the long-term survival of elephant populations.

Biological Diversity Act, 2002

This act was established to ensure the conservation of biological diversity, promote its sustainable use, and ensure fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of genetic resources.

India's Protected Area Network

National Parks

Strictly protected areas designated by the central government. They are established to protect an entire ecosystem, including its flora, fauna, and landscapes. Human activities like forestry, grazing, and hunting are not permitted.

Wildlife Sanctuaries

Areas of significant ecological value where conservation efforts are focused on protecting specific species. Certain human activities may be allowed on a regulated basis, as long as they do not interfere with wildlife.

Biosphere Reserves

Large, protected landscapes that include national parks and wildlife sanctuaries. They are designed to conserve biodiversity while promoting sustainable use of natural resources and fostering research and education.
